PHASE 8d, PER DAY STORM ONSET PROBABILITY AFTER A POP
==============================================================================

PRESENTATION ONLY. This note records what one figure draws. No statistical test was run, no skill score or verdict was computed, no existing number changed, and no finding was added. The study's answer remains F7 (SPEC 8.4, DECISIONS.md).

Why the figure exists: the project's lag scan (F3) is a WINDOWED skill score, and a window can smear a sharp, narrowly timed signal by averaging its target days with neighbours that carry none. This is the direct per day view of the same claim, asked for by a reader. Either outcome would have been useful; the check is made directly rather than argued from the windowed result.

Exploration winters used (16): [1989, 1990, 1992, 1994, 1996, 1998, 1999, 2000, 2001, 2003, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020]
  include_holdout was never set True. The six held-out winters (2004, 2005, 2006, 2008, 2021, 2022) were not loaded, plotted or counted (rule 2.3).
  Predictor column: buoy_51001_wvht_mean

DEFINITIONS, imported from the locked rule and not re-chosen (SPEC 8.2)
  pop day    : buoy_51001_wvht_mean >= 3.9530 m (fixed metre value)
  storm day  : swe_gain_in >= 0.5 in at >= 2 reporting stations (any2)
  events     : detect_events, bridge = 1 day, dated by first day
  storm onset: the first day of a storm event, so a multi day storm has exactly one onset day and cannot contribute to several lags at once
  anchors    : pop anchors are pop event first days; non pop anchors are every other day outside every pop event with a defined pop flag (Q25, rule 2.5)

Anchors: 144 pop events and 2537 non pop days at lag 0. The denominator at each lag counts only anchors whose day L is still inside the same winter, so it shrinks towards the long lags and at the season edges.
  Cross check against numbers already on record, not a new measurement: at lag 18 the denominators are 142 pops and 2263 non pop days, which is the same in season condition the locked rule's window imposes. Phase 5a recorded 142 usable pop occasions at this configuration (DECISIONS.md Q16, F2) and F7 quotes 2263 exploration comparison occasions (c + d = 1438 + 825). The two match, so the anchors here are the study's own occasions counted a different way, not a different sample.

CAPTION, as written onto the figure:

  Probability that a storm begins on each day after a buoy pop, out to 30 days, on the
  exploration winters, against the per day baseline (the background chance a storm begins
  that many days after any non-pop day). The folklore predicts a bump around 12 to 14 days,
  shaded. The pop line stays close to the baseline across the whole range and the cited
  range is no exception: it averages 0.0794 there against a baseline of 0.0864, that is,
  below the background rather than above it. Stated plainly because it is visible: the
  smoothed line's own highest point does fall at lag 14, at 0.0983 against a baseline of
  0.0878, but it is a single point about one percentage point above the background, it sits
  beside lag 12, which carries the lowest raw value in the entire range at 0.0420, and the
  neighbouring days do not rise with it. That is a wiggle, not a bump. Isolated single day
  wiggles are expected here because each point rests on only tens of pop events (140 to 144
  across the lags, shown beneath), each carrying a handful of storm onsets.

  Locked definitions (SPEC 8.2): pop = daily mean wave height at buoy 51001 >= 3.9530 m;
  storm = SWE gain >= 0.5 in at >= 2 reporting SNOTEL stations (any2); both collapsed into
  events by detect_events, bridge = 1 day, dated by first day, so a multi day storm has one
  onset. Anchors whose day L would fall past 30 April are dropped from that lag rather than
  counted as no storm.

  A true signal would show a broad rise above the baseline sustained across several adjacent
  days near the cited lag, not a lone spike. This is a descriptive visualisation, not a
  test, and adds no finding: the study's answer remains F7. Exploration winters only,
  EXPLORATORY.
